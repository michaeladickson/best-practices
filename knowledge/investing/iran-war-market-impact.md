# Iran War — Market & Energy Impact

**Date:** 2026-03-23
**Source:** Goldman Sachs ISG Client Call, "The Third Week of War in the Middle East" (March 18, 2026)
**Speakers:** Rear Admiral Mike Studeman (former ONI), Norman Roule (former CIA/ODNI Iran), Brett Nelson (ISG Tactical Asset Allocation)
**Status:** Active — conflict ongoing

## Timeline

- **Feb 28, 2026**: Operation Epic Fury begins — US and Israeli strikes on Iran
- **By March 17**: US has struck 7,000+ targets (missiles, drones, naval forces, defense industrial base, regime security)
- Iranian daily launch activity declined from 800 (Feb 28) to ~30 (March 17)
- Israel eliminated 50+ senior Iranian leaders including Supreme Leader Khamenei

## Casualties (as of March 17)

- **Iran**: 1,444 killed, 18,551 injured (Iranian state media)
- **US**: 13 killed, 200 injured
- **Israel**: 15 killed, 3,000+ injured
- **Regional**: 76 killed, 3,400+ injured across Gulf states + Lebanon

## Strait of Hormuz

- Traffic through the Strait down 90%+ from prior levels
- Middle Eastern producers forced to shut in production as storage fills
- Pipeline rerouting + SPR releases replace only ~half of the 20M bbl/day that normally transits
- Only durable solution: resumption of tanker traffic
- Houthis have NOT yet intervened — significant escalatory potential remains
- 2 of 3 US minesweeping ships sent to Malaysia

## Oil Market

- **Largest supply disruption in history by volume** (~8M bbl/day)
- Second-largest as % of production
- WTI up 43%, Brent up 44%, European natural gas up 61% since Feb 27
- Global visible oil inventories provide short-term buffer
- Oil intensity 60% lower than 1973 — US less vulnerable than historical shocks
- US is now a net energy exporter (was net importer in 1990 and earlier)

## Equity Market Impact

- S&P 500 down 2%, Europe down 8%, EM down 7%
- Gold up 6%, 10-year Treasury up 2%
- Historical pattern: equities typically absorb oil spikes (95% positive 8 weeks later after past MENA strikes)
- **But**: persistent multi-month oil price elevation correlates with worse equity outcomes
- Key difference vs past shocks: today's macro backdrop is healthier (lower inflation, moderate Fed policy, no credit crunch)

## Key Uncertainties

- Will Strait of Hormuz reopen in weeks or months?
- Houthi intervention potential
- Iranian regime stability post-decapitation of leadership
- Nuclear material status ("under the rubble" per Iranian FM)
- US ground operations? (31st MEU with 1,200 Marines en route)

## Investment Implications

- Energy prices are the key variable — duration of disruption matters more than initial spike
- US net exporter status + lower oil intensity = structural resilience vs past shocks
- Macro backdrop healthier than 1990/2022 — no concurrent credit crunch or inflation crisis
- If Strait reopens in weeks (per US Energy Secretary): likely transient shock, equities recover
- If disruption persists months: amplifies economic stress, worse equity outcomes
- Gulf state assets most exposed; US least exposed among developed markets
- Gold performing as expected safe haven
- Monitor: Strait traffic resumption, Houthi status, ceasefire negotiations

## Relevance to Portfolio

- Energy sector exposure provides natural hedge
- US equities more resilient than European/EM due to energy independence
- Duration of oil shock is the key variable to monitor, not the initial spike
- Consider: is the 2% S&P decline sufficient pricing for tail risk, or does the market expect quick resolution?
